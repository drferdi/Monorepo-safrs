The logomark in the system

Two filled paths measured from the original artwork: one accent bar, and one continuous body that climbs, turns, and returns. The bar is the one that takes colour. Everything on this page governs where that colour is allowed to appear, not how the mark is drawn.

Exempt from the icon spec

UI icons are butt-capped and 1.5 stroke. The mark is filled outline, not stroke, and its ends are cut horizontally. Brand geometry is fixed and never redrawn to an internal rule. Brand geometry is fixed and is never redrawn to match an internal rule.

Provenance of this geometry

Traced from logo.png · 97.06% match

This is a trace, not the source. The SVG embedded in the product-logo file was a reconstruction: 13.8% wrong on aspect ratio, 11% wrong on stroke weight, and split into three loose pieces. It was discarded. The geometry here was measured directly from logo.png and fitted until it overlapped the original by 97.06% of pixels. It is close, and it is honest about being close. When the vector source appears, replace this and delete the paragraph.

And the stroke swells at the bend, 77.2 to 86.5, the classic optical correction that stops a curve reading thinner than the straight it joins.

The finding

Measured against both canvases

All five product accents sit at the same lightness. They are 1.00–1.01 apart from one another — authored as siblings, which is why they read as one family. The cost is that every one of them lands between 2.11 and 2.95 on white, below the 3:1 that a meaningful graphic requires. The palette is not wrong. It is a dark-canvas palette.

03

Two ways out

One preserves the brand exactly

Option 1 — the mark keeps its own ground

Recommended. The mark sits on an ink square wherever it appears, on any canvas. Accents stay exactly as authored — 6.32 to 8.86 — and the brand never drifts. The square also gives the mark a consistent optical size, which three loose bars on open white never have.

Option 2 — darken for white

The mark sits directly on white with darkened accents. It breathes more, but the colours are no longer the brand's — a designer opening Figma and this screen side by side will find two different greens. Every new product would need two values maintained forever.

06

Where it does not belong

Needs a written exception
  • On data panels — record inspectors, crawl logs, gate tables
  • As a watermark behind content, at any opacity
  • Repeated on every row of a list or table
  • In any colour that is not an authored product accent
  • Carrying status — the mark says which product, never how it is doing
  • Without the product name in text beside it
  • Below 20px, or above 96px
  • Rotated, stretched, recoloured bar by bar, or redrawn to match the icon spec

The five accents are 1.00 apart from each other. In greyscale, in print, or to a colour-blind operator they are one colour. That is acceptable — a product colour never has to be told apart from another product's colour, because only one product is on screen at a time, and on the launcher every card carries its name. Colour confirms recognition here. It is never asked to carry it.
